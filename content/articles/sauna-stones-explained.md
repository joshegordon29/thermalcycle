Sauna stones are the heat battery of a traditional sauna.

The heater produces heat. The stones store it, shape it, and turn water into steam. If the stones are wrong, old, packed too tightly, or stacked poorly, the room can feel flat even when the heater is strong.

## What Sauna Stones Do

Good sauna stones hold heat and tolerate thermal shock. Thermal shock is the repeated stress of getting very hot and then receiving water.

Ordinary decorative rocks are not automatically safe or useful. Sauna stones are chosen because they can handle the cycle.

## Common Stone Types

Olivine diabase is one of the common standards for sauna heaters. [Harvia](https://www.harvia.com/en-US/products/AC3000/sauna-heater-stones-510-cm-20-kg) describes olivine diabase as a common stone for sauna heater use. Peridotite is also used and is often discussed as a dense, durable option. Exact recommendations depend on heater manufacturer guidance.

Follow the stove maker's stone size and loading instructions first.

## Size Matters

Small stones expose more surface area and can create lively steam, but they can also restrict airflow if packed too tightly. Larger stones leave more air gaps but may respond differently to water.

Most heaters specify a stone size range. Use it.

## Stacking

Do not pack stones like concrete. Air needs to move through the heater. Place stones loosely enough for airflow while still covering the heating elements or fire path as intended by the manufacturer.

Bad stacking can shorten heater life and weaken the room.

## When To Replace Stones

Replace stones when they crack, crumble, produce grit, or stop performing well. In home use, inspection a few times a year is reasonable. Commercial use needs more frequent attention.

If the room takes longer to heat or steam feels dull, the stone load is one of the first things to check.

## The Bottom Line

Good stones make the room feel responsive. Bad stones make a strong heater feel tired.
