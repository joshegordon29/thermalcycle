Sauna wood has to handle heat, moisture swings, bare skin, and repeated cleaning. The best choice depends on budget, smell, availability, and how you want the room to age.

Cedar, hemlock, and thermally modified woods are common for good reasons. They are not interchangeable.

## Cedar

Cedar is popular because it is stable, aromatic, and visually warm. It has a strong identity. People often know they are in a cedar sauna before they sit down.

The aroma is either a feature or a problem. Some people love it. Others want a quieter room.

Choose cedar if you want the classic North American sauna look and smell, and if the budget allows for clear, high-quality boards.

## Hemlock

Hemlock is usually lighter in color and less aromatic than cedar. That makes it a good choice for people who want the heat experience without a strong wood scent.

It can be more cost-effective, but quality matters. Clear boards, good milling, and proper installation are more important than the species name alone.

## Thermowood

Thermowood is thermally modified wood. The [International ThermoWood Association](https://thermowood.fi/thermowood) describes the process as heat and steam modification without added chemicals. The goal is better dimensional stability and durability.

Thermowood often has a darker, more uniform look. It can feel more contemporary than cedar or pale hemlock.

## Bench Feel

Bench material matters more than wall material because your skin touches it. Avoid woods that splinter easily, heat too sharply, or have resin problems.

Whatever species you choose, the bench should be smooth, stable, and comfortable under bare skin.

## The Simple Rule

Choose cedar for aroma and tradition. Choose hemlock for a quieter, lighter room. Choose thermowood for stability and a more modern material story.
