Infrared and traditional saunas are different heat experiences.

A traditional sauna heats the room air, benches, stones, and surfaces. An infrared sauna uses infrared emitters to warm the body more directly, usually at lower air temperatures.

That difference changes the feel of the session.

## Traditional Sauna

Traditional sauna is the reference point for hot-room culture: high heat, benches, room air, and often water on stones.

The experience is environmental. You are inside a heated room, and the whole room matters. Ventilation, bench height, ceiling height, stones, and heater placement all shape the session.

## Infrared Sauna

Infrared saunas usually operate at lower air temperatures. The heat can feel more direct and less room-based.

Some people like the lower-temperature entry point. Others miss the room heat, steam, and social architecture of traditional sauna.

## Which Gets Hotter?

Traditional saunas typically run hotter in terms of air temperature. Infrared cabins can still feel intense because the heat is delivered differently.

Do not compare only the number on the thermometer. Compare the actual experience.

## Buying Considerations

For traditional sauna, look at heater quality, ventilation, bench layout, wood, and stone load. For infrared, look at emitter layout, cabin materials, electrical requirements, and whether the seating position exposes the body evenly.

In both cases, avoid miracle claims. Buy the room, not the marketing.

## Which Is Better?

Choose traditional sauna if you want heat, steam, room feel, and the classic cycle of hot air and cooling. Choose infrared if you want a lower-temperature cabin that is easier to place indoors.
