Felted wool works in a sauna because it traps air.

That sounds too simple, which is usually where good materials start.

## The Useful Layer

When wool is felted, the fibers lock together into a dense mat. Inside that mat are tiny pockets of air. Those pockets slow heat transfer.

In the sauna, that means the hottest air does not hit your scalp and ears with the same bite.

## Better Than Synthetic Shells

Synthetic materials can trap heat in ways that feel clammy or strange. Wool breathes, handles moisture, and belongs in a hot room without needing much explanation.

It is not futuristic. It is proven.

## What Good Felt Feels Like

Good felt has body. It should not collapse immediately, but it should not feel like armor either.

For a sauna hat, the goal is steady heat, not total separation from the room.

## Why Thickness Matters

Thin felt can look right and still fail in the room. The useful part is density. You want enough material to slow the heat without making the hat heavy, wet, or awkward.

That balance is the difference between a sauna object and a sauna prop.
