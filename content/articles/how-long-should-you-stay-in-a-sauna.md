Most people want a number. The better answer is a range.

For many sauna users, a round lands somewhere between 8 and 20 minutes, depending on heat, humidity, bench height, experience, hydration, and how the body feels that day.

## Start Lower Than Your Ego Wants

If you are new, start with a shorter round. Leave while you still feel clear. Rest outside the room. Drink water. Then decide whether you want another round.

The second round is often better because your body knows what is happening.

[Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/saunas-and-your-health) gives a conservative safety rule of no more than 15 to 20 minutes for many sauna users, along with drinking water afterward. That is a useful ceiling for general writing, not a challenge to reach.

## Watch The Right Signals

Heat should feel strong, not confusing. Leave if you feel dizzy, nauseous, chilled, disoriented, or like you are negotiating with yourself. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/heat-exhaustion/symptoms-causes/syc-20373250) lists dizziness and nausea among heat exhaustion warning signs, which is a good reminder not to treat those signals as normal sauna discomfort.

No stopwatch is smarter than your body.

## Bench Height Changes Everything

The upper bench can be dramatically hotter than the lower bench. If you want a longer round, move lower before you force yourself to leave.

Staying in the sauna is not the goal. Having a good session is the goal.

## A Better Rule

Leave before urgency. Rest longer than you think. Go back in only if you want the heat again.
