What you wear in a sauna depends on the room, the rules, and the culture around it.

The material rule is simple: wear as little synthetic material as the setting allows, avoid metal hardware against skin, and use a clean towel or seat layer in shared rooms.

## Public Sauna

Follow the posted rules first. Some public saunas require swimsuits. Some require towels. Some have gender-specific or clothing-optional traditions.

If swimsuits are required, choose a simple suit without metal trim, heavy padding, or complicated straps.

## Private Sauna

In a private sauna, a towel is often enough. Sit on a clean towel, keep another nearby for drying, and avoid bringing unnecessary clothing into the room.

The less fabric you bring in, the less fabric needs to handle heat and sweat.

## Infrared Sauna

Infrared cabins often run lower in air temperature, and many users wear light workout-style clothing. If you do, choose breathable pieces and wash them immediately after.

Avoid clothes with prints, coatings, or hardware that may not belong in repeated heat.

## Sauna Hats

A sauna hat is not clothing in the normal sense. It is heat equipment. If your head gets hot first, a wool hat can make the round feel more even.

For the basics, read [What Is a Sauna Hat?](/articles/what-is-a-sauna-hat/).

## What To Avoid

Avoid metal jewelry, heavy synthetic layers, shoes in the hot room unless required for accessibility or facility rules, and anything you would not want heated against your skin.

The best sauna outfit is clean, simple, and appropriate to the room.
